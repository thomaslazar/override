# Specials and boosters in popular match-3 games

Date: 2026-11-18. Purpose: let the owner pick new specials, pre-level gear and in-level tools for Override.

Method and limits. Fandom wikis refused automated fetching (HTTP 402), so this survey rests on web search
results: publisher help centres (Dreamgames, Playrix, King, AWEM), BlueStacks and PocketGamer guides, and the
Bejeweled wiki. Facts marked (?) are single-source or sources disagree; confirm in game before copying a number.
In-game currency prices were mostly not visible in sources; none are invented here. The Playwright browser
was not needed for the claims below, so no screenshots were taken.

## 1. Per-game tables

Common shape language: line of 4 = line clearer, L/T = area blast, line of 5 = colour clearer, 2x2 square = small
homing or blast piece (Dreamgames and Playrix style).

### Royal Match (Dreamgames), Royal Kingdom (same studio, same rules, per Apptipper/BlueStacks)

| Special | Made by | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Rocket | 4 in a line | clears the row (or column) |
| Propeller | 2x2 square | flies to a tile with many targets, hits it (and neighbours) |
| TNT | L or T of 5 | large blast (radius about 2, sources differ) |
| Light Ball | 5 in a line | clears one colour |

Pairs: Rocket+Rocket cross (row and column); Rocket+TNT three rows and three columns; TNT+TNT bigger blast (4
to 5 radius, (?)); Propeller+Rocket/TNT the propeller carries it to the best target and fires it there;
Propeller+Propeller three propellers; Light Ball+Rocket/TNT/Propeller converts the most common tiles into that
power-up and fires them; Light Ball+Light Ball clears the board and strips one obstacle layer.

Pre-level boosters: a free-standing Rocket placed at start, plus the other starting power-ups (Royal Hammer,
Arrow, Cannon, Jester Hat are sold as in-game boosters; one source files them as pre-level, (?)). Several can
be taken at once. Earned from chests, events and level rewards, or bought with coins; one third-party site says
1 coin per use for the four tools (unverified). A coin pack ranges $0.99 for 100 coins up to $99.99 for 14,000.

In-level tools: Royal Hammer (one tile or one obstacle layer), Arrow (a row), Cannon (a column and one obstacle
layer), Jester Hat (shuffle). Using them does not spend a move.

Goal targeting: the Propeller picks the tile with the highest concentration of targets, favouring goal pieces
and obstacles; random among ties.

### Candy Crush Saga and Soda (King)

| Special | Made by | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Striped candy | 4 in a line | clears a row or column (direction follows the match) |
| Wrapped candy | L or T | 3x3 blast, falls, 3x3 blast again |
| Colour bomb | 5 in a line | clears one colour (the colour it is swapped with) |
| Jelly fish (Saga) | 2x2 (?) | three fish seek goal pieces |
| Coloring candy (Soda only) | special combo | recolours things (Soda-specific) |

Pairs: Striped+Striped cross; Striped+Wrapped a very wide stripe (three rows and columns); Wrapped+Wrapped a
5x5 blast then a bigger one; Colour bomb+Striped turns that colour into stripes and fires all; Colour bomb+Wrapped
clears that colour then a second colour with a blast (sources differ, (?)); Colour bomb+Colour bomb clears the
board.

Pre-level boosters: Lucky Candy (a lucky piece placed at start), Striped and Wrapped (start with one of each),
Extra Moves x3 (gift or purchase), Coconut Wheel/Jelly Fish and others by level type. Earned by daily spin,
events, friends, bought with gold bars. In-level: Lollipop Hammer (smash one candy or blocker), Free Switch
(swap any two adjacent for no move), Extra Moves after failure (+5 moves; one source says 10 gold bars,
rising with repeat failures).

Targeting: fish fly to goal pieces first (jelly, ingredients), else random.

### Bejeweled (PopCap)

## 2. Recurring patterns

1. Four shapes carry nearly every game: 4-line (line clear), L/T (blast), 5-line (colour), 2x2 (small seeker).
   Override has the first three. The 2x2 seeker is the obvious gap and is what the owner's rockets are.
2. Two specials swapped give a bigger version of both; the colour piece plus anything converts that colour.
3. Seekers (propeller, plane, fish) pick goal pieces first, obstacles second, random last, and are the main
   answer to "last piece stuck in the corner".
4. Pre-level boosters are almost always one of: a free special placed at the start, a free special pair, or extra
   moves. They are earned from chests and events, bought with coin, and capped at a few per level.
5. In-level tools: single-cell remove (hammer), swap without move, row or column clear, shuffle, extra moves after
   a loss. Most cost no move; extra moves cost currency and rise with repeat failure.
6. Targeting is almost never player-chosen for specials, always for tools.

## 3. Fits Override

Rules for all items: a drop is never destroyed; encrypted cells are cracked one layer by any hit, as a blast does
today; a firewalled tile is unlocked (not removed) by a hit. Holes and blocks stop line travel at the edge for
reaching purposes only where the existing worm does; the rocket flies, so it ignores them. Teleporters are
ignored by blasts (as today). Complexity ratings are rough (small is about a day of core work).

### Candidate specials

1. Rocket seeker (the owner's idea), made by a 2x2 square. On firing it spawns 3 rockets. Each picks a target
   in this order: (a) unlocked-goal tiles (a firewalled tile of the goal colour), (b) encrypted cells when
   cracking is a goal, (c) tiles of a remaining tile-goal colour, (d) the drop nearest the drop point is
   skipped (never destroyed) but a tile directly above a drop is eligible so the drop falls, (e) random tile.
   Targets are distinct; a rocket flies in a straight visual arc (animation only), then clears its cell as a
   one-cell wave (cracks, unlocks, pops, as any wave). Seeded by the rocket tile id so replays are the same.
   Holes: cannot be a target. Teleporters: ignored. Combos: rocket + rocket = 8 rockets (not 6; the bonus makes
   the swap worth a move); rocket + worm: each of 3 rockets carries a worm and fires it on arrival (3 lines);
   rocket + bomb: 3 mini blasts of 3x3 on arrival; rocket + zero-day: convert colour to rockets, fire all (cap
   at 12 rockets, extras become single pops). Complexity: medium (new special, target picker, one step type).
2. Cross worm (combine two worms already makes this; promote to a 5-wide made by T of 5): skip, overlaps bomb.
3. Mine (2x2 in a different place, passive), a tile that, when cleared, blasts 3x3 later: large, skip.
4. Colour seeker variant: "locator", made by a line of 6, clears every tile of the goal colour that is
   not in a hole; effectively zero-day aimed. Overlaps zero-day, small. Probably skip.
5. Chain bolt, made by two overlapping L's (a 5-cell W): jumps between 5 random tiles of the board's most
   common colour. Medium.
6. Shield breaker, made by a 6+ line: unlocks every firewalled tile on the board and cracks every encrypted
   cell once. Strong for unlock goals, no effect on plain boards. Small to medium.
7. Teleport swap, made by a square of 3x3 ring (8 cells): swaps its cell with any chosen cell, ignoring
   adjacency (like Free Switch as a special). Medium (fairness: swap legality and bot).
8. Gravity worm, a 4-line variant when the line crosses a spawner: also pushes tiles toward the spawner. Large,
   skip.
9. Prism (5 in a line crossing an L): clears the three most common colours at once. Medium, overlaps zero-day.
10. Echo, made by two 3-matches of one colour in one move: next match this move also fires one worm. Large.

Recommendation: 1 (rocket seeker), 6 (shield breaker), 5 (chain bolt), 4 (locator) in that order; others skip.

### Pre-level gear (picked before the level, capped at 2 slots)

1. Starter worm: places one worm at a seeded safe cell. Small. Never on a drop, hole, block or firewall.
2. Starter rocket seeker: same, with item 1 above. Small after the rocket exists.
3. Spare moves (+3): raises the limit. Small. Interacts with nothing; the bot limit is only the base.
4. Starter zero-day: one zero-day on the board. Small. Fires as normal; its random colour is never a drop's.
5. Cracked start: removes one encrypted layer on two cells (or unlocks two firewalls). Small to medium; only
   offered on levels that have them, so no dead slot.
6. Magnet: biases the first refill so one drop falls one row closer (drop levels only). Medium. Skip unless
   drops prove too hard.

Earning: levels give a gear piece on first clears; limited stock keeps it scarce. Prices stay out of scope for now.

### In-level tools

1. Hammer: clear one tile, free of move cost. A drop is not clearable (refuse), a block is not clearable,
   encrypted cell loses one layer, firewalled tile unlocks. Small.
2. Free swap: swap any two adjacent tiles, no match needed, no move cost. A hole, block, or drop cannot be
   swapped. Small to medium (swap legality code).
3. Shuffle: reshuffles non-special, non-drop, non-locked tiles; guarantees a legal move. Small; core already
   has a no-move shuffle, so likely reused.
4. Line clear: pick a row or column, fires as a worm wave from the edge without using a move. Small; reuses
   worm firing.
5. Extra moves (+3 or +5 after a loss, cost currency, rising with fails). Small; fits the existing `fails`
   logic.
6. Rewind: undo the last move once per level. Medium to large (needs state history; the core is pure so a
   snapshot copy is cheap).

Suggested first slice: rocket seeker (special), starter worm plus spare moves (gear), hammer plus shuffle
(tools). Reason: all reuse existing waves, and each is small.
